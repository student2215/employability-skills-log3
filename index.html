About this log

This professional log records the mandatory employability skills I developed during the BSM24005 group project, where my team researched a contemporary issue and a cultural trend and their impact on a creative industry sector. Each skill below is linked to a specific module activity and to my future career goals. Photographic evidence from my independent research is included in the gallery.

Mandatory employability skills

01

Time-management & prioritisation

Evidence: I built a shared schedule for the group project and divided research tasks by week, so the Q&A preparation, the reflection, and this log were each started early rather than left to the deadline.

Developed through: Planning the group Q&A and balancing it against the individual reflection and skills log.

02

Persistence, following instructions & trustworthiness

Evidence: I read the assessment brief closely and kept my research aligned with the chosen contemporary issue and cultural trend. When early sources were weak, I returned to the library and digital databases until I found reliable material.

Developed through: Independent research in the library and on LSST Connect, shown in the gallery.

03

Meeting commitments & deadlines

Evidence: I delivered my section of the group research on time and submitted each assessment task before its deadline, so the group was never held back by my part.

Developed through: The three staged deadlines across the module (Q&A, reflection, skills log).

04

Willingness to learn

Evidence: I used academic texts and the module's digital resources to understand issues such as AI, sustainability, and representation in the creative industries, and I applied this reading to our group argument.

Developed through: Engaging with the core and recommended reading and the module's e-resources.

05

Internal & external self-awareness

Evidence: I recognised that I tend to procrastinate under pressure, so I set earlier internal deadlines for myself. I also asked for feedback from group members to see how my contribution was viewed.

Developed through: Reflecting on my role using Gibbs' reflective cycle.

Reflection (Gibbs' Reflective Cycle)

Description

During the BSM24005 group project I researched our chosen contemporary issue and cultural trend, met every deadline, and used both library books and digital resources independently to gather reliable evidence.

Feelings

I felt nervous about the workload at first, but I became more motivated and confident once our tasks were divided clearly.

Evaluation

My time-management and commitment to deadlines went well, although my early self-awareness about procrastination was a weakness I needed to manage.

Analysis

Structured reflection helped me interpret these experiences, as Gibbs (1988) argues that genuine learning comes from examining feelings and actions rather than description alone. Cottrell (2015) adds that employability depends on evidencing such transferable skills.

Conclusion

I learned that prioritisation and persistence directly strengthened my contribution to the team.

Action plan

Next time I will set earlier internal deadlines and seek feedback sooner to develop my professional practice.

References
Cottrell, S. (2015) Skills for Success: Personal Development and Employability. 3rd edn. London: Palgrave Macmillan.
Gibbs, G. (1988) Learning by Doing: A Guide to Teaching and Learning Methods. Oxford: Further Education Unit.
